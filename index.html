    YOUR COURTSIDE FIELD GUIDE

    A few things that
    make it click.

    01

    Rotation ≠ playing position

    The zones run 1 (right back), 2 (right front), 3 (middle front), 4 (left front), 5 (left back), 6 (middle back). After winning the right to serve, rotate 2 → 1 → 6 → 5 → 4 → 3 → 2.

    02

    One setter or two?

    A 5–1 uses the same setter in all six rotations. A 6–2 uses two opposite setter/hitters: the back-row player sets and the front-row player attacks. This demo uses that no-substitution version.

    03

    Wait for serve contact

    Receiving positions are relative, not fixed zones. A back-row player can stand near the net while keeping the required relationship with their front-row counterpart and back-row neighbors. Officials compare feet. Once the server hits the ball, release into playing positions; do not wait for the first pass.

    04

    Your row still matters

    A back-row setter can move to the net to set, but cannot block or complete an above-net attack after taking off in the front zone. Moving forward does not make them a front-row player.

    05

    The libero’s exchange

    Here, the libero replaces the back-row middle. Before that slot reaches the front row, the middle returns. The other middle can then be replaced in the back row. Real exchanges must follow competition timing rules; this lesson condenses them.

    06

    Learn one rally at a time

    Compare Serve receive with Rotation order. In the order view, playback first moves into a legal receive formation, then the server hits at 0:03. Each example ends in a side-out. The next lesson assumes you are receiving again; serving rallies in between are omitted.
